# Rwanda Cultural Knowledge Base (v0, internet-sourced)

This file grounds the LLM's item-generation prompts for the Rwanda locale. It is a first-pass
knowledge base built from public web sources (see citations at the bottom of each section), not
from live local teacher/student interviews. Treat it as a strong starting point, not verified
fact — a local teacher review pass is recommended before any generated item goes live with real
students. Do not let the LLM add cultural details beyond what is written here.

## Games & play (informs Game-based trait items)
- Igisoro: two-player board strategy game, 32-pit wooden board, 64 seeds/stones, players count
  seeds and follow strict turn rules to capture the opponent's seeds. Widely regarded as Rwanda's
  signature heritage game (similar family to mancala games played elsewhere in Africa).
- Guhana Ubute ("passing laziness"): a tag/chase game — one player touches another to pass a
  token, and whoever is left holding it at the end is teased good-naturedly.
- Gusimbuka Urukiramende: high jump over a rising crossbar, individual skill competition.
- Sebutimbiri: jumping over a sequence of low bars (~50cm) consecutively, individual skill.
- Gukirana: traditional wrestling without striking; group/competitive game.
- Intembe: archery through a moving banana-trunk-ring target; group skill game.
- Gusimbuka umugozi: rope jumping, sometimes synchronized in pairs; group game.
- Children in rural areas making toy cars and other objects out of wire.
- Note: several sources describe some of these as historically split by gender (e.g. archery
  associated with boys, doll play/stone-catching with girls). Use these as a pool of mechanics
  and flavor for a mixed modern classroom — do not gender-restrict which student an item mentions
  playing which game.
[Sources: allafrica.com "Top 10 Rwandan Traditional Games"; Google Arts & Culture "Games and
Recreation in Rwanda"; makeheritagefun.com "Igisoro"]

## Oral storytelling (informs Story-based trait items)
- Imigani: folktales/fables with animal characters that have human traits, mythical beings, or
  ordinary people in extraordinary situations. Structured narratives (clear beginning/middle/end)
  built around moral themes — courage, justice, cunning, how intelligence beats brute strength,
  how greed leads to loss.
- Ibisakuzo: riddles, traditionally posed in playful group settings to spark discussion and
  critical thinking.
- Proverbs (Imigani Migufi): short, metaphorical statements encapsulating traditional wisdom.
- Traditionally shared at family gatherings/evenings; today also carried through schools, radio,
  TV, and digital platforms.
[Source: hobenews.com "Imigani and Ibisakuzo: The Fabric of Rwandan Oral Tradition"]

## Visual arts & crafts (informs Visual Exploration trait items)
- Imigongo: bold geometric art (spirals, triangles, curves) in black/white/terracotta-red,
  traditionally made from cow dung mixed with natural pigment on wood panels, used to decorate
  house walls; originally from Rwanda's eastern region.
- Agaseke ("peace baskets"): tall, conical woven baskets in intricate geometric/spiral patterns,
  natural fiber (sisal) dyed in black/brown/white tones; symbolize peace, generosity, and harmony;
  traditionally exchanged at weddings or as important gifts.
- Also present: pottery, beadwork (bright symmetric patterns for jewelry/dance regalia), and
  woodcarving (masks, figurines).
[Source: gorillatrekkingexpeditions.com "Rwanda's Traditional Arts and Crafts" — tourism-oriented
source; good for visual/descriptive flavor, lower confidence for precise cultural claims]

## Community & structured activity (informs Structured trait items)
- Umuganda: mandatory monthly community work day (last Saturday of the month), with a clear
  structure — assigned tasks (cleaning, building/infrastructure work) followed by a community
  meeting to discuss local issues. Something virtually every Rwandan child has direct experience
  with, and a strong anchor for "everyone has a role, steps happen in order" scenario framing.
- Igisoro's rule sequence (count seeds before playing, strict turn order, specific capture
  conditions) also has a procedural/structured flavor and can double as a Structured-mode
  reference, not just a Game-mode one.
[Sources: globalsistersreport.org; borgenproject.org; kizastore.com]

## Public primary school context (informs the Recommendation-stage locale context, not items)
- Pupil-teacher ratio improved from 61:1 (2017) to 44:1 (2021) nationally, but with real
  disparities — trained-teacher ratio averages 57:1, reaching 120:1 in some areas.
- Primary 1-2 classes are most crowded (73:1-78:1 in 2021).
- 64% of primary schools run double shifts; double-shift students get ~22 instructional
  hours/week vs. ~42 for single-shift — a hard real constraint on classroom time.
- English became the official instructional language in 2008, but only 38% of lower-primary
  teachers meet English teaching standards, and ~90% of students can't read a grade-level English
  text — a strong signal to use Kinyarwanda for lower-primary assessment items.
- No solid data found on classroom ICT/technology availability specifically — assume low/no
  per-student device access as the safe MVP default.
[Source: UNICEF "Education in Rwanda: A situation analysis", January 2024]
